WATER PURITY DETECTION SYSTEM

Aug - Dec 2024

To address the limitations of stationary water-quality sensors and slow manual sampling, I developed a bio-inspired soft robotic underwater drone capable of autonomously monitoring pH levels in real time. Clean-water access is a growing global concern, and this project contributes by enabling dynamic environmental sensing in areas conventional probes cannot reach. I designed the soft-robotic structure, integrated pH sensors with IoT hardware, and programmed the navigation and data-collection system in Python. The prototype demonstrated stable and repeatable pH measurements with less than 5% drift while significantly improving spatial coverage through mobile sensing. The results show that a soft-robotic platform can provide effective, continuous water-quality surveillance, offering a promising alternative to fixed monitoring stations.

02

DEEPFAKE VOICE DETECTION

Jan - May 2025

Deepfake audio poses serious risks to security and privacy, motivating the need for more robust detection systems. In this project, I built a reinforcement learning–based neural architecture search (NAS) framework to automatically design models for deepfake voice detection. I implemented a complete audio-processing pipeline—including log-mel feature extraction—and trained baseline CNN models in PyTorch, achieving a strong Equal Error Rate (EER) of 2.8% after 50 epochs. I then integrated a policy-gradient RL controller to explore new model architectures and evaluate their performance. This system not only achieved state-of-the-art baseline performance but also began identifying more parameter-efficient architectures. My role covered the full workflow from preprocessing and model design to RL integration and evaluation, demonstrating the effectiveness and potential of NAS for advancing audio deepfake detection.

03

SLAM SIMULATION ENVIRONMENT

Aug 2024 - May 2025

Mining sites are hazardous, making real-world SLAM experimentation costly and risky. To support safe autonomy research, I created a high-fidelity Isaac Sim environment for evaluating SLAM algorithms in wheel-loader hauling and loading operations. I designed complex terrain, dynamic obstacles, and realistic mining layouts, and integrated ROS2 communication along with LiDAR and stereo camera simulations. This environment produced high-quality sensor data for testing mapping, localization, and multi-sensor fusion algorithms under repeatable conditions. By replacing dangerous real-mine testing with virtual experimentation, the system proved highly effective for early-stage SLAM development. I was responsible for building the full simulation world, configuring sensors, scripting operations, and validating the data pipelines.

GRAPH NEURAL NETWORKS FOR ENGINEERING DRAWINGS

May 2025 - Present

Engineering drawings are essential for manufacturing but require tedious manual interpretation, which introduces opportunities for error. To streamline this process, I am developing graph neural network (GNN) models that classify drawings and automatically identify important geometric features. I reviewed state-of-the-art GNN research, converted technical drawings into graph-based representations, and implemented prototype models using PyTorch Geometric. Early results show promising accuracy in recognizing structural features and relationships across varying drawing styles, indicating strong potential for automated design review tools. My role includes literature review, dataset creation, model development, and ongoing experimentation as part of broader research into intelligent engineering-drawing analysis.

05

PEDICLE SCREW FIXATION DESIGN

Jan - May 2023

Pedicle screws play a critical role in spinal surgeries, where poor fixation can lead to severe complications. To support better implant design, I conducted finite element studies analyzing how variations in screw geometry and material affect stress distribution in vertebrae. I modeled different screw configurations in SolidWorks, applied realistic loading conditions, and evaluated stress patterns through FEA simulations, using MATLAB for post-processing and visualization. The study showed that titanium designs reduced peak stresses and provided more uniform load transfer, offering guidance for safer and more reliable spinal implants. I carried out the modeling, simulation execution, and comparative analysis, demonstrating the effectiveness of computational tools for improving orthopedic device performance.
